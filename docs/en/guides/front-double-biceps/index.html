Front double biceps: repeating a clear line

To compare this pose, keep the same viewpoint and correct one visible difference at a time: arm height, shoulder alignment or hip stability.

Set up your reference

  1. Place the iPhone at a distance that keeps your whole body in frame, even when your elbows open out.
  2. Keep the camera in the same spot every session and avoid backlight that hides your joints.
  3. Hit the position facing the lens, check the relative height of your elbows and shoulders, then hold the line without rushing.
  4. Compare one take at a time. If an angle changes, fix it before redoing the whole pose.

How PoseLock helps here

The app tracks body points with Vision and gives feedback on the angles it can detect. When the hold threshold is reached, it keeps a local photo for the journal. This feedback is not a judge’s mark: it depends on how visible your limbs are and on the pose model. A hand or elbow out of frame makes it less reliable.

To build a complete routine, start with the bodybuilding posing guide. If nobody can hold the phone for you, see how to practise alone.

Frequently asked questions

Should I stand close to the camera?

No. What matters is that your arms, hands, legs and feet stay visible throughout the pose.

Does a higher score mean a better placing in a show?

No. It only shows that the detected landmarks are closer to the model the app uses, in the conditions of that take.
