Getting started with bodybuilding posing

Posing is not just about taking a flattering photo. It means repeating a line, holding a position and checking what changes from one take to the next.

A short session in four steps

  1. Pick a single pose. Repeating it in the same conditions makes the comparison more useful than switching poses every attempt.
  2. Frame your whole body. Place the iPhone far enough away to see your head, hands and feet without leaving the frame.
  3. Hit the position, then hold it. Watch the visual feedback to correct one angle at a time, without trying to move your whole body at once.
  4. Compare after the session. Look at the overall line, symmetry and stability, then note the correction to work on tomorrow.

What the score can — and can’t — tell you

PoseLock compares body landmarks detected by Vision with a pose model. It helps you repeat more consistent framing and angles. It does not measure muscle mass, the quality of a prep or a panel’s opinion. Lighting, clothing, occlusion and framing can change the detection.

Start with a simple pose, then move on to the front double biceps once your setup is stable. If you train without a partner, follow the method for posing alone with an iPhone.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a coach to start?

Not to repeat and observe your position. A coach is still useful for technical feedback or contest prep; the app does not replace one.

Why repeat the same pose?

Because a comparison only makes sense if the pose, framing and distance stay close from one session to the next.
